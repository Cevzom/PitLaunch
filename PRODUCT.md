# PitLaunch Product

## Register

product

## Users

PitLaunch is for a PC enthusiast who uses one Windows machine in two physical contexts: a normal desk and a sim-racing rig. They open it at startup or during a transition and want to choose a setup without thinking through individual monitor, audio, application, or power settings.

## Product Purpose

PitLaunch captures the PC's real device and window state as reusable profiles, then restores a chosen profile with one action. Success means the active setup is always obvious, switching feels immediate and trustworthy, and editing a saved profile never requires technical knowledge of Windows device identifiers.

## Brand Personality

Focused, trustworthy, and modern. The app should feel like a polished Windows utility built for an enthusiast: quiet during configuration, decisive during switching, and never theatrical.

## Anti-references

- Windows utilities that look inherited from 2009.
- Fullscreen choice screens with no way to understand or edit what a mode contains.
- Dense forms that expose internal device details before the user needs them.
- Decorative dashboard cards, neon gaming themes, or motion that delays a switch.
- Ambiguous buttons or duplicate active-state information.

## Design Principles

1. Make the next action obvious. Choosing or reapplying a setup is the primary workflow.
2. Capture reality. Prefer detected displays, devices, processes, and files over manual transcription.
3. Show state before controls. The user should immediately know which setup is active and what each profile contains.
4. Earn trust during system changes. Use plain progress and partial-failure feedback, and preserve access to logs without making them prominent.
5. Keep expert power progressive. Common switching stays simple; hardware and automation detail are one level deeper.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Support keyboard navigation, clear focus states, readable contrast, and labels that do not rely on color alone. Motion remains brief and functional, with no bouncing or long entrance sequences. Text and controls must remain usable at Windows display scaling and at the app's minimum window size.
